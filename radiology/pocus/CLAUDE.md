# pocus
Purpose: point of care ultrasound simulator. A virtual adult on a table, a probe you place and angle with your hands (slide, rotate, fan, rock, press), and a machine with the controls of a real one (probe and exam preset, depth, gain, TGC, frequency, focus and zones, dynamic range, power, persistence, speckle reduction, edge, grey map, THI, freeze and cine, calipers, M-mode, zoom, flips). The image is simulated from the physics of sound in tissue, live, with breathing and a beating heart. Cases (trauma, tamponade, pneumothorax, shock and more) and a scored FAST, eFAST and RUSH exam sit on top.
Input: nothing to load. The patient is a procedural model, there is no scan or manifest.   Output: `index.html`, a single page.
Done when: every standard window can be found with the probe and shows what it should (`windows.test.js`), depth, gain, TGC, frequency, focus and orientation visibly change the image, a FAST exam can be taken and scored, and the node tests pass.
Run: serve `radiology/` over http and open `pocus/index.html`. Dev views: `dev.html?from=right&a=12&z=-130&phi=105&rock=-20&fan=25&depth=180&set=fluidRUQ:10&labels=0.6`. Photograph pages in headless Chromium with `node radiology/tools/pocus-shot.mjs --out <dir> --shots '{...}'`, and add `?still=3` to the app page so it settles after three frames.

## Files
- Physics, pure and tested: `tissues.js` (impedance, attenuation, backscatter of each tissue), `beam.js` (wavelength, pulse length, focused beam width, THI, frame rate, MI and TI), `probes.js` (linear, curvilinear, phased geometry), `geometry.js` (probe pose on the skin, `solvePose`), `anatomy.js` (the shape engine and its JS evaluator), `visibility.js` (what a pose can see, without drawing).
- The patient: `patient.js` is the table of about 300 shapes (body wall, ribs, spine, lungs, heart with valves, liver, gallbladder, kidneys, spleen, stomach, bowel, aorta, IVC, pelvis, bladder), its parameters (`DEFAULTS`) and the named structures. Everything that moves or changes with disease is a function of those parameters.
- GPU: `gl.js` helpers, `anatomy-glsl.js` (the table interpreter, generated from the JS constants), `shaders.js` (the passes), `sim.js` (the pipeline, the cine ring, M-mode), `patient-view.js` (the 3D view with its own context).
- Machine and teaching, pure and tested: `machine.js` (state, presets, knob to simulator numbers), `motion.js` (cardiac and respiratory cycles), `view.js` (screen to probe mm), `measure.js` (calipers), `cine.js`, `scenarios.js` (the cases), `windows.js` (the standard views and what each needs) with generated `windows-poses.js`, `exam.js` (recognising a view, quality, score).
- Screen: `app.js` (wiring and the clock), `screen.js`, `console.js`, `widgets.js`, `probe-panel.js`, `exam-panel.js`, `pocus.css`.

## Gotchas
- Coordinates are mm in the module's LPS frame, as the viewer: x to the patient's left, y posterior, z superior. The origin is the midline at the xiphisternal joint, the patient lies supine and the table is the plane y = 115. Never flip an image outside `view.js` and `mirrored()`: screen right is the probe's `e` axis, so a transverse plane shows the patient's left on the right, as in a CT. Abdominal presets put the probe marker on the left of the screen, cardiac on the right.
- The anatomy is resolved every frame into a flat float table (`resolve` in `anatomy.js`, nine texels per shape). The JS evaluator and the shader interpreter read that same table, and the body profile and rib tables are written into the shader from the JS constants, so there is one copy of each number. Painter's order matters: a later shape wins, with a soft edge of half a millimetre so the impedance step can be differentiated. A shape can be clipped by two others.
- Look at the anatomy before trusting it. `node radiology/tools/anatomy-slice.mjs --out <dir> --axial=-40 --coronal=30` draws tissue-coloured cuts. After any change to `patient.js` run `node radiology/tools/make-windows.mjs` (about 30 minutes, it can run in the background) to rewrite `windows-poses.js`, then the tests. `--only a4c,luq` redoes some. `tools/aim.mjs` holds the heart plane seeds and `tools/window-gallery.mjs` photographs every window.
- Image formation, per pass (see `shaders.js`): find where each line meets the skin and whether gel couples the face, sample the anatomy and draw scatterers from world-fixed noise (so speckle moves with the tissue, and with each organ's own breathing motion), reflection from impedance steps weighted by the angle of the surface, a running sum of loss down each line (so shadows and enhancement are the physics, not drawn), the pulse and the beam width as filters, lung and gas artefacts hung off the first air interface of each line, then noise, depth compensation, TGC, dynamic range and the grey map. Two things are added after depth compensation because they are not echo from tissue and must not be lifted by it: the near field haze (`CAL.veil`) and the haze below air, which is set as a ratio to the pleural line. Echo amplitude 1 is the mean echo of liver. The only tuning constants are in `CAL` in `sim.js`, and the clutter and lateral factors in `beam.js`.
- Honest limits, and the page says the simulation is educational: sound speed is constant (no refraction), speckle re-randomises when the probe moves faster than the beam width, there is no Doppler yet, and the heart's contraction is a smooth scripted motion rather than a physical model.
- Needs WebGL 2 with `EXT_color_buffer_float`. Software GL (the test browser) works and takes seconds per frame and about 40 seconds to compile shaders, so test pages must use `?still=N`.
- `Alt` and `Ctrl` shortcuts never fire. Arrow keys slide the probe unless a slider, knob or select has focus.
- Real scan data was not used. The public datasets in `../data` are single regions with thick slices, different patients, and no pelvis, so they cannot give one body with every FAST window. If a licensed whole body labelled CT becomes available, the engine can take it as another patient (it only needs impedance, attenuation and backscatter per voxel).
See: `../docs/pocus-content.md` for the shapes of cases and windows, `../viewer/CLAUDE.md` for the orientation rule this follows.
